Introduction

APGAR Score – A Quick Assessment of the Newborn

Developed by Dr Virginia Apgar in 1952, published 1953
Standardized documentation of the newborn's condition right after birth
5 parameters, each scored 0, 1 or 2 → total 0–10
Clinical message ▸
Routinely assessed at 1 and 5 minutes. A simple scoring system with an important role in neonatal assessment and documentation.

Timing

Timing Is Everything

1 min: initial condition, early response to delivery and transition.
5 min: ongoing condition; response to initial interventions.

If the 5-minute score is below 7: repeat every 5 minutes until 20 minutes of age.

RESUSCITATION MUST START WITHOUT WAITING FOR APGAR.

Limits

What APGAR Can and Cannot Tell Us

Factors that may influence the score

  • Prematurity / immature neuromuscular function
  • Maternal medications (opioids, anesthetics)
  • Congenital abnormalities
  • Maternal or fetal medical conditions
  • Respiratory or neurological compromise
  • Resuscitation interventions

Limitations

  • Not designed to predict individual neurological outcome
  • Cannot independently diagnose birth asphyxia
  • Not the sole indication to start resuscitation
  • Subjective; influenced by circumstances
Clinical pearl ▸ A low APGAR score does not necessarily mean intrapartum hypoxia or asphyxia.
It documents condition at a moment in time – it does not explain the cause.

Documentation

Beyond the Conventional APGAR

In newborns requiring resuscitation, the conventional score may not fully describe the situation. The expanded APGAR documents concurrent interventions.

Interventions
  • Supplemental oxygen
  • Positive-pressure ventilation
  • Endotracheal intubation
  • Chest compressions
  • Medications
Documentation
  • Record scores at 1 and 5 min
  • If 5-min < 7: every 5 min to 20 min
  • Document interventions with the score
  • Consider umbilical arterial blood gas if 5-min score ≤5 (ACOG/AAP), in clinical context

Summary

Take-Home Messages

  • Five parameters, each scored 0–2
  • Assess at 1 and 5 minutes
  • If 5-min < 7, repeat every 5 minutes up to 20 minutes
  • Never delay resuscitation to calculate the score
  • A low score alone does not diagnose birth asphyxia
  • Interpret in context of clinical condition and interventions
